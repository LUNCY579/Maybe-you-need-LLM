# Licensing, attribution and originality

This document records, in one place, exactly what this project contains, where it
came from, and under what terms it may be used.

---

## 1. The source

The mathematics taught by this application comes from one monograph:

> Daniel A. Roberts, Sho Yaida, and Boris Hanin,
> *The Principles of Deep Learning Theory: An Effective Theory Approach to
> Understanding Neural Networks*,
> Cambridge University Press, 2022.
> Preprint: **arXiv:2106.10165** — <https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10165>

### Its licence, and what it does not grant

The arXiv preprint carries a **non-exclusive distribution licence** and is
associated with a **Cambridge University Press** publication. Public availability
of a preprint is a grant to *read* it, not a grant to republish it. In particular:

- The book's text, figures, tables, exercise sets, typesetting and layout are
  protected expression and are **not** reproduced here, in whole or in part.
- No image, diagram, figure caption or extended passage from the book appears in
  this project, in any medium.
- The book is used as a **cited scholarly reference**. Where the application
  implements a mathematical result, the result is cited by chapter, section and
  equation number (for example `eq. (5.55)` for the two criticality conditions).

### What *is* used from the source

Mathematical **facts**: definitions, recursions, closed forms, scaling laws and
their domains of validity. Reproducing a mathematical statement and attributing
it is standard scholarly practice, and is what the assignment to build an
interactive teaching tool on this book requires. The attribution is deliberately
granular: every lesson carries a `citations` list, every equation entry carries a
`ref`, and every laboratory panel displays citation chips naming the exact
equations its chart is about.

## 2. What is original to this project

Everything except the mathematics itself was written for this project:

| Asset | Status |
| --- | --- |
| All lesson prose, intuition, worked examples and exercise answers | Original |
| All equation-explorer text: symbol glossaries, assumption lists, derivation walk-throughs, substitution examples | Original |
| All quiz questions, answer keys and explanations | Original |
| All experiment hypotheses, descriptions and expected outcomes | Original |
| All figure code, chart layouts, dashboards, laboratory controls, visual system | Original |
| All numerical code: MLP, analytic backpropagation, Gauss–Hermite and bivariate quadrature, criticality scanner, optimizers, linear algebra, seeded RNG, PCA, Fisher ratio, datasets, persistence | Original |
| The two-moons, circles, Gaussian-cluster and regression datasets | Own constructions from their parametric definitions, generated by a seeded RNG in this repository. No dataset from the book or any other source is used. |
| The visual identity (dark palette, typography, layout) | Original |

No automated or manual extraction of the book's prose, figures or tables was
performed for this project. The PDF was read as a reader would read it: as a
reference for mathematics.

## 5. A note on the Cambridge University Press edition

The monograph was published commercially by Cambridge University Press. The
arXiv preprint and the CUP edition are not interchangeable: the preprint is a
draft, and the published edition contains text that this project has not seen. If
you need the authoritative statement of a result, consult the CUP edition. The
equation numbering used throughout this project follows arXiv:2106.10165v2, which
is the version this project was written against.

## 6. Attribution you are asked to carry

If you reuse the lessons, the equation-explorer entries or the laboratory code,
please keep the attribution visible. The in-app **Sources & licensing** screen
(`/#/sources`) states the attribution chain in the product itself, and the
`SOURCE` object in `src/content/references.ts` is the single place where the
citation is stored.
